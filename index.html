Learn the motion basics

Everything here is pulled from your distilled study guide, just cleaned up and organized so it’s easier to remember.

Motion

Motion + reference point

Motion is a change in position compared to a reference point. A reference point is the thing you compare motion to.

Relative motion

Depends on the observer

Relative motion means motion can look different depending on who is observing it. What looks still to one observer can look like motion to another.

Vectors

Magnitude + direction

Vectors have both size and direction. That matters for velocity because velocity is not just a number — it tells where the object is going too.

Speed

How fast, no direction

Speed is magnitude only, with no direction. Units: m/s. Formula: speed = distance ÷ time.

Velocity

Speed with direction

Velocity is speed with direction. Units can look like m/s north. In your notes: velocity = change in distance ÷ change in time.

Acceleration

Change in velocity over time

Acceleration tells you how velocity changes over time. Units: m/s². Formula: (vf - vi) ÷ t.

When is something accelerating?

Acceleration can happen when an object is speeding up, slowing down, or changing direction.

Distance vs. time graph

Straight line = constant speed
Steeper line = faster
Curved up = accelerating
Flat line = stopped
Slope = speed

Velocity vs. time graph

Flat line = no acceleration
Slanted up = speeding up
Slanted down = slowing down
Slope = acceleration
