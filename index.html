The concepts, explained like a good teacher would

These are your same science ideas, just translated into memorable language so you’re not trying to memorize random vocabulary five minutes before class.

motion

Motion is basically “did your location change?”

If you were sitting on a bench and now you’re walking toward the pier, that’s motion. In science language, motion is a change in position compared to a reference point.

Memory hook: no comparison point = no way to prove motion. Motion needs a “compared to what?”
reference point + relative motion

Reference point = your “anchor” for judging movement

A parked lifeguard tower can be your reference point. If you move away from it, your position changes. And because different observers can use different reference points, relative motion means motion can look different depending on who’s watching.

Think train analogy: to someone inside, you might look still; to someone outside, you’re moving fast.
vectors

Vectors are numbers with attitude

A vector has magnitude and direction. So it doesn’t just say “how much” — it says “how much and where.” That’s why velocity is a vector but speed is not.

Magnitude answers “how much?” Direction answers “which way?” Put them together and you get a vector.
speed

Speed is the plain version

Speed only tells how fast. No direction. Just the amount. If a car is going 20 m/s, that’s speed. Formula: speed = distance ÷ time.

Dashboard analogy: speed is what your speedometer says. It doesn’t tell you where you’re going.
velocity

Velocity is speed with receipts

Velocity includes direction. So 20 m/s east is velocity, not just speed. Your notes frame it as change in distance ÷ change in time, but the key thing to remember is the direction piece.

If speed is “how fast,” velocity is “how fast and toward where.”
acceleration

Acceleration is any change in velocity

A lot of people think acceleration only means speeding up. It doesn’t. If you speed up, slow down, or turn, your velocity changed — so that is acceleration. Formula: (vf − vi) ÷ t.

Acceleration = change. If the motion story changes, acceleration happened.

Make it memorable

If you blank during the formative, these are the kinds of mental shortcuts that can pull the answer back into your brain.

graph mindset

Distance vs. time = “How far did I get?”

This graph is about position changing over time, so the slope tells you speed.

Flatter line = slower. Steeper line = faster. Flat line = literally not moving. Curving upward = getting faster, so accelerating.
graph mindset

Velocity vs. time = “What is my motion doing?”

This graph tracks velocity itself, so the slope tells you acceleration.

Flat line = no acceleration. Slant up = speeding up. Slant down = slowing down.
